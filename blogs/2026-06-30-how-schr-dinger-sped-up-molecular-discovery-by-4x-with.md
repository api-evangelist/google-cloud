Computational chemistry researchers have traditionally faced a frustrating trade-off when simulating molecular interactions: use fast classical force fields that sacrifice precision or rely on accurate quantum-mechanical methods that run too slowly on large jobs. Machine-learned force fields (MLFFs) close that gap by training neural networks on high-fidelity quantum data. When it comes to modern drug discovery and materials design, though, there’s demand for even faster processing speeds to handle massive chemical libraries involved.
